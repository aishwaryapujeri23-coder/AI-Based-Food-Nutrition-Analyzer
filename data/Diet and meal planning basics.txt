Basics of Diet Planning and Meal Planning

This note explains the general concepts behind personalized diet planning
and meal planning, which pairs with the calculators in this application.

Understanding BMR and TDEE:
- BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate) is the number of calories your body needs at
  complete rest just to maintain basic functions like breathing and
  circulation.
- A common way to estimate BMR is the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, which uses
  weight, height, age, and gender.
- TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) takes BMR and multiplies it by an
  activity factor to estimate how many calories you burn in a typical day,
  including exercise and daily movement.
- Activity factors typically range from about 1.2 (sedentary, little or no
  exercise) up to about 1.9 (very intense training or physically demanding
  jobs).

Setting a calorie target:
- To lose weight, a modest deficit below TDEE (commonly around 15-20%) is
  generally more sustainable than a very large deficit.
- To maintain weight, calorie intake is set close to TDEE.
- To gain muscle or weight, a modest surplus above TDEE (commonly around
  10-20%) combined with resistance training supports lean gains rather
  than mostly fat gain.

Macronutrient splits:
- Protein: supports muscle repair and growth, and is particularly
  important during weight loss (to preserve muscle) and muscle gain (to
  build it). Roughly 25-35% of calories from protein is a common range
  depending on goal.
- Carbohydrates: the body's primary energy source, especially for
  higher-intensity exercise. Whole, minimally processed carbohydrate
  sources are generally preferred over refined ones.
- Fat: supports hormone production and nutrient absorption. Healthy fats
  from nuts, seeds, oils, and fish are preferred over trans fats and
  excessive saturated fat.

Building a day of meals:
- Distributing calories across 3-5 meals (breakfast, lunch, dinner, and
  optional snacks) helps manage hunger and energy levels throughout the
  day.
- Each meal ideally includes a protein source, a fiber-rich vegetable or
  fruit, and a portion of whole-grain or other complex carbohydrate,
  adjusted based on individual calorie and macro targets.
- Snacks, when included, are best chosen to add protein or fiber (such as
  nuts, roasted chana, yogurt, or fruit) rather than empty calories.
- Meal plans should also be adapted for dietary preference (vegetarian,
  vegan, non-vegetarian) and any medical conditions, since some conditions
  require additional adjustments beyond general calorie and macro targets.

This information is educational and general in nature. Extreme or very
low-calorie diets should not be undertaken without guidance from a doctor
or registered dietitian.
