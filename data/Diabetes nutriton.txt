Nutrition Guidance for Type 2 Diabetes

Type 2 diabetes is a condition where the body does not use insulin effectively,
leading to elevated blood sugar levels. Diet plays a central role in managing
blood glucose alongside any prescribed medication.

Key principles:
- Favor low glycemic-index carbohydrates such as oats, quinoa, brown rice,
  and whole legumes over refined grains and sugar. These raise blood sugar
  more slowly and steadily.
- Build meals around a combination of fiber, lean protein, and healthy fat.
  Pairing carbohydrates with protein or fat slows glucose absorption and
  reduces post-meal spikes.
- Eat consistent meal times and portion sizes where possible, since large
  irregular meals make blood sugar harder to control.
- Include plenty of non-starchy vegetables (leafy greens, broccoli, peppers,
  cucumber) which add fiber and nutrients with minimal impact on blood sugar.
- Limit sugary beverages, fruit juice, and refined sweets, which cause rapid
  spikes in blood glucose.
- Watch portion sizes of starchy staples such as rice, potatoes, and bread.
- Nuts, seeds, and fatty fish provide healthy fats and can help improve
  insulin sensitivity over time.
- Fenugreek seeds and cinnamon are sometimes used as complementary additions,
  though they are not a substitute for medication or medical advice.

Practical tips:
- Reading nutrition labels for "added sugar" and "total carbohydrate" helps
  when comparing packaged foods.
- Smaller, more frequent meals can help some people avoid large swings in
  blood sugar compared to two or three very large meals.
- Regular physical activity, especially after meals, helps the body use
  glucose more effectively.

This information is educational and general in nature. People with diabetes
should work with their doctor or a registered dietitian to set individual
carbohydrate targets and medication timing, since these vary significantly
from person to person.
