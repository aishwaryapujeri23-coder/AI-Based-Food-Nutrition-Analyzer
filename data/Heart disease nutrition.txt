Nutrition Guidance for Heart Disease

A heart-healthy diet focuses on managing cholesterol, blood pressure, and
inflammation, all of which influence cardiovascular risk over time.

Key principles:
- Replace saturated fats (found in butter, ghee, and fatty cuts of meat)
  with unsaturated fats such as olive oil, mustard oil, nuts, and seeds
  where possible.
- Avoid trans fats entirely. These are found in many fried foods and
  packaged baked goods and are strongly linked to heart disease risk.
- Include soluble fiber sources such as oats, barley, and legumes, which
  can help lower LDL ("bad") cholesterol.
- Eat fatty fish such as salmon, mackerel, or sardines a couple of times a
  week for omega-3 fatty acids, or use plant sources like flaxseed and
  walnuts if you don't eat fish.
- Increase vegetables, fruits, and whole grains, which provide fiber,
  antioxidants, and potassium that support cardiovascular health.
- Limit sodium to help manage blood pressure, a major risk factor for
  heart disease.

Foods and habits to limit:
- Deep-fried foods and foods with hydrogenated oils (trans fats).
- Excess red meat and processed meat (sausages, salami, bacon).
- Full-cream dairy products in large amounts.
- Sugary drinks and desserts, which contribute to weight gain and elevated
  triglycerides.

Practical tips:
- Small swaps add up: use olive oil instead of butter, choose grilled or
  baked over fried, and pick whole-grain over refined-grain versions of
  bread, rice, and pasta.
- Portion control matters as much as food choice; even healthy fats are
  calorie-dense.
- Regular physical activity works together with diet to improve
  cholesterol and blood pressure.

This information is educational and general in nature. People with
diagnosed heart disease should follow the specific dietary and medication
plan set by their cardiologist or dietitian.
