Nutrition Guidance for Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)

Nutrition needs in chronic kidney disease vary greatly depending on the
stage of disease and whether a person is on dialysis. General principles
often discussed include limiting sodium, potassium, and phosphorus, and
adjusting protein intake, but exact limits must come from lab results and
a renal dietitian, not general guidance.

General themes often seen in CKD nutrition education:
- Sodium is usually limited to help control blood pressure and fluid
  retention, similar to hypertension guidance.
- Potassium may need to be limited in later stages, since impaired kidneys
  struggle to remove excess potassium from the blood. Lower-potassium
  vegetables and fruits (such as cauliflower, cabbage, bell peppers,
  apples, and berries) are sometimes favored over higher-potassium options
  like bananas, oranges, and potatoes — but this depends entirely on an
  individual's lab values and stage of disease.
- Phosphorus may also need to be limited in later stages, since it can
  build up in the blood when kidney function declines. Processed foods
  with phosphate additives are often a bigger concern than natural food
  phosphorus.
- Protein intake recommendations vary significantly: some earlier-stage
  patients are advised to moderate protein, while dialysis patients often
  need more protein, not less. This is one of the most individualized
  aspects of CKD nutrition.
- Cooking techniques such as soaking or boiling certain vegetables can
  reduce their potassium content before eating.

Why individualization matters:
- CKD nutrition needs shift substantially by stage (1 through 5) and by
  whether someone is on hemodialysis, peritoneal dialysis, or not yet on
  dialysis.
- Lab values (potassium, phosphorus, albumin, and others) drive real
  dietary limits far more than generic food lists.

This information is general and educational only. CKD nutrition should
always be guided by a nephrologist and a renal dietitian using an
individual's specific lab results and treatment plan — general food lists
are not a substitute for personalized medical guidance in this condition.
