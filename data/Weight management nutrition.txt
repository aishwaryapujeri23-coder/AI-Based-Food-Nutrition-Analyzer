Nutrition Guidance for Weight Management

Sustainable weight management is generally best achieved through a modest,
consistent calorie deficit (for weight loss) combined with adequate protein
and fiber to control hunger and preserve muscle mass.

Key principles:
- A calorie deficit of roughly 15-20% below maintenance (TDEE) supports
  steady, sustainable fat loss without being so extreme that it is hard to
  maintain or causes excessive muscle loss.
- Prioritize protein at each meal (lean meat, fish, eggs, paneer, tofu,
  legumes). Protein is more filling than carbohydrates or fat and helps
  preserve lean muscle during weight loss.
- Fill meals with high-fiber vegetables and whole fruits, which add volume
  and nutrients with relatively few calories, helping you feel satisfied.
- Choose whole grains over refined grains in controlled portions rather
  than eliminating carbohydrates completely, which is rarely sustainable
  long term.
- Stay hydrated with water and unsweetened beverages; drinks are a common
  source of "invisible" calories.

Foods and habits to limit:
- Sugary drinks, desserts, and packaged sweets, which add calories quickly
  without much satiety.
- Deep-fried and highly processed foods, which are calorie-dense and easy
  to overeat.
- Very large portions of otherwise healthy foods — portion size matters
  even for nutritious meals.
- Alcohol, which provides "empty" calories with little nutritional value.

Practical tips:
- Tracking portions (even loosely, using hand-size estimates or a food
  scale for a few weeks) helps build awareness of typical serving sizes.
- Aim for gradual, sustainable weight loss of about 0.3 to 0.5 kg per week
  rather than rapid loss, which is more likely to be maintained long term.
- Combining diet changes with regular strength training helps preserve
  muscle mass while losing fat.
- Sleep and stress management also affect hunger hormones and are worth
  paying attention to alongside diet.

This information is educational and general in nature. People with other
health conditions should check with a doctor or dietitian before starting
a new weight-loss plan.
